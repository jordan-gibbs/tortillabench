PROCEDURAL-PHOTOREAL-01 — TECHNICAL NOTES

Modeling and scale
The script creates one connected 22.4 cm circular dough sheet from a quad grid
mapped to a disc. Trigonometric edge variation avoids a perfect cut-out outline.
The render surface retains the baked cloth positions, then adds subdivision,
fine procedural blister relief and approximately 1.8–2.3 mm of uneven thickness.
Normal-based solidification avoids the long miter spikes found in the first
iteration. The cut rim has its own pale 3D crumb shader so collapsed rim UVs do
not produce streaked bump normals.
The cutting board is a beveled solid block, 34 x 26 x 2.1 cm.

Physics
A Cloth modifier folds the original disc twice. Sequential animated shape-key
pin targets describe finite-radius cylindrical hinges. Distributed hard grips,
soft interior pin weights and less constrained boundary vertices guide the
motion while the cloth solver handles drape, gravity and collisions. This is a
guided cloth simulation, not an unconstrained gravity-only folding experiment.
The undeformed rest mesh stays fixed. Board collision and self-collision are on.
The simulation uses 10x spatial scale for thin-sheet numerical stability, then
converts the baked snapshot and board to metres for the macro camera. A small
rigid vertical contact correction after adding thickness seats the lowest
surface at the board. The value is disclosed in simulation.json.
The scene saves both the source animation/Cloth modifier and an evaluated final
mesh so the photograph does not depend on cache re-evaluation when reopened.

Shading
All materials are built from procedural shader nodes. Original disc UVs keep
cooking marks attached to the dough through both folds. Voronoi cells with noisy
boundaries and varying radii create toast marks, broad noise varies dough colour,
and high-frequency noise supplies flour grains, roughness and micro-normal
relief. Subsurface scattering gives a restrained warm translucency. The board
uses strongly anisotropic noise for longitudinal wood grain and a separate
crossing pattern of sparse shallow knife scratches with a satin oil finish.
No external models, textures, HDRIs, add-ons or asset libraries are used.

Photography and rendering
A low 76 mm macro camera at f/5.6 focuses on the leading layered edge. Sparse
fine toasted freckles and flour mottling complement the larger griddle spots.
A nine-bladed aperture
softens the rear of the board and the quiet charcoal studio background. A large
warm area key, restrained cool fill and soft rear strip reveal fold volumes.
World illumination is a constant procedural shader. Cycles uses a fixed seed,
GPU compute with CPU fallback, denoising, AgX and a 16-bit PNG output.

Reproduction
The definitive run command, iteration critique, output paths, runtime and
hardware are recorded with the completed results in iteration_log.json and
REPORT.txt. build_tortilla.py also embeds a copy of itself in the saved scene.
